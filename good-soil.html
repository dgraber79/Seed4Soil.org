"But other seed fell on good ground and yielded a crop that sprang up, increased and produced: some thirtyfold, some sixty, and some a hundred." — Mark 4:8 · NKJV
"But these are the ones sown on good ground, those who hear the word, accept it, and bear fruit: some thirtyfold, some sixty, and some a hundred." — Mark 4:20 · NKJV
"To you it has been given to know the mystery of the Kingdom of God." Mark 4:11 · NKJV

The good soil hears the Word with a noble and good heart, keeps it, and bears fruit with patience. (Luke 8:15) The soil is open, deep, and uncontested — it receives the seed and gives itself fully to what the seed needs. The relationship between seed and soil is the one both were made for — spacious, deeply attached, loving, and exclusive — it ends in fruit neither could have produced alone. Here, the mystery of God's kingdom is finally known.
