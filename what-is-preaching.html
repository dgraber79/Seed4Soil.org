What is "Preaching?"

Biblical preaching always involves proclaiming God's Word through Logos and/or Rhema expressions.

The Greek New Testament defines preaching as making an official announcement or public declaration. The essential consideration becomes determining what content warrants such proclamation.

Ancient prophets announced messages the LORD placed upon them, proclaiming divine instruction. Jesus similarly proclaimed words His Father provided. Mark 1:14–15 records Jesus announcing: "The time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God is at hand. Repent, and believe in the gospel." John 14:24 emphasizes this pattern: the spoken words originated from God the Father.

Logos vs. Rhema — Word of God vs. Word of Man

The written scriptures constitute the Logos Word. When God spoke temporally to prophets and Jesus, that utterance represented Rhema — God's specific spoken word for particular moments. Contemporary preachers proclaiming from scripture present Logos. When the Spirit inspires contemporary application beyond written text — addressing specific situations — this represents Rhema spoken for that context, though never contradicting scripture. Personal human interpretation without divine inspiration simply constitutes human speech.

What then is preaching in the Biblical sense?

"Biblical preaching always involves proclaiming God's Word through Logos and/or Rhema expressions."

They trust the Spirit's illumination to reveal contemporary Rhema — personal or communal applications — always consistent with written scripture. Any conflicting teaching represents a different voice.
