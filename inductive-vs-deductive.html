Inductive Logic versus Deductive Logic

"Inductive logic starts with observation, moves to interpretation (exegesis), and finishes in application" based on multiple observations that yield probable conclusions.

"Deductive logic draws conclusions from observations of the Bible or natural world by using logical or mathematical reasoning."

Inductive Bible Study

"An inductive Bible study approach starts with a Bible passage and attempts to understand what it is saying before drawing any conclusions."

Key features include emphasizing observation first, openness to revised conclusions with new evidence, and discovery-based methodology.

"Personal ownership through discovery is key for the seed of God's Word to enter into the heart and encounter the right conditions to grow to fruit bearing."

Deductive Bible Study

"A deductive Bible study approach starts with a particular principle, teaching premise, assumption, or deduction and backs it up with supporting Bible passages."

Typically organized topically, deductive studies compile proof texts supporting a predetermined doctrine. The approach can restrict discovery and personal ownership compared to inductive methods.

"Deductive Bible study is a useful tool if we recognize its limitations. It is potentially hurtful in that it can sabotage discovery and personal ownership."

Inductive

  • Starts with observation of the text
  • Moves to interpretation (exegesis)
  • Finishes in application
  • Conclusions open to revision
  • Fosters personal ownership through discovery

Deductive

  • Starts with a principle or premise
  • Backs it up with Bible passages
  • Typically topically organized
  • Can restrict discovery
  • Useful if we recognize its limitations
