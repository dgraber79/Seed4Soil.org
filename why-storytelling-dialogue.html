Storytelling and dialogue are powerful educational methods rooted in Jesus's own teaching approach.

The Power of Storytelling

"The storyteller sets the vision, values and agenda of an entire generation." Jesus used narrative as his primary teaching method, impacting billions across history.

Jesus's Teaching Methods

Jesus employed stories, questions, and examples rather than direct textual references. He asked roughly twice as many questions as he answered. Most biblical content is narrative and story-based.

"Jesus taught using stories, examples, and questions most of the time in His teaching as opposed to lecturing. He created space for people to hear and choose whether or not to respond." He was "a master of storytelling and dialogue."

The Value of Dialogue Questions

Dialogue questions enable discovery-based learning instead of passive reception. They respect listener dignity and foster ownership of ideas. They promote curiosity and openness to God's Word.

The Importance of Listening

The primary command in the greatest commandment is "Hear" (Mark 12:29–30, Deuteronomy 6:4). Listening equals knowing, which leads to loving. It requires genuine connection and understanding.

"HEAR, O Israel: The LORD our God, the LORD is one. You shall love the LORD your God with all your heart, with all your soul, with all your mind, and with all your strength." Mark 12:29–30 · NKJV
