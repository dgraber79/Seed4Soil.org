Frequently Asked Questions

Key questions about the Parable of the Sower, Seed4Soil, and how to engage God's Word for transformational growth.

Seed4Soil exists to see people encounter the living Word of God and be changed by it. We train disciples and disciplemakers in oral, story-based Bible engagement — accessible methods rooted in how Jesus himself taught — so that communities can hear Scripture, experience it together, and respond.
"Do you not understand this parable? How then will you understand all the parables?" Jesus asked. For God's reign (kingdom) to be on this earth, God's Words need to enter good soil, according to Jesus.
Jesus tells us: "The sower sows the Word." That seed is God's living Word — the same Word that "became flesh and dwelt among us" (John 1:14).
"And the LORD God formed man of the dust of the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living being." Humans are made in God's image from soil, and soil serves as the receptacle for the Seed of God's Word.
"To you it has been given to know the mystery of the kingdom of God; but to those who are outside, all things come in parables." Those "coming close, hearing, and responding" can understand.
How can the Living Word of God grow in a body made of soil and bear fruit? How can the Word become flesh in my soil? How can a human life become the place where a good God lives and works? All this happens and more, and this is the mystery that our Lord wants to reveal to us. Will you come and see?
The parable begins and ends with a command: "HEAR!" Good soil "hears the word, accepts it, and bears fruit." The good soil "comes to Jesus, hears His Word, and does it."
Jesus taught using stories, examples, and questions most of the time in His teaching as opposed to always lecturing. He created space for people to hear and choose whether or not to respond. He was a master of storytelling and dialogue.
Then you are exactly who this is for. You don't need to be gifted, trained, or eloquent — you need one story told faithfully. Every storyteller struggles with the same things at first: fear of silence, forgetting parts of the story, slipping into religious language, feeling you must say something clever. These are normal, and they fade with practice. The power was never in the teller — "the seed is the Word of God" (Luke 8:11), and the seed does its own work. Start with the Ten-Minute Story Learning Method and one listener.
