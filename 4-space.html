"The Lord is in His holy temple. Let all the earth keep silence before Him." Habakkuk 2:20 · NKJV

Imagine being interrupted mid-sentence — repeatedly — by someone who isn't really listening. Now imagine the opposite: someone who asks you a question, then waits, then listens, then asks one more. You know the difference in your body. The first leaves you feeling invisible. The second leaves you feeling seen.

The same difference exists in how we give God's Word to others. Strategy 4 is the practice of building a room where God can speak and be heard. That room has four elements.

Silence

Silence Builds the Walls

Before God spoke creation into existence, the Spirit hovered in silence. The Temple in Jerusalem was built without the sound of a hammer — stone cut and shaped before it arrived, so that the sacred space itself was never broken by noise. Elijah did not hear God in the wind, the earthquake, or the fire — but in a still small voice that came only after all the noise had passed.

The Word cannot work in noise. Silence is not emptiness — it is the wall that protects God's Word from being drowned out. What cannot be heard cannot be obeyed.

Listening

Listening Builds the Floor

Jesus' first recorded act of personal initiative was sitting in the temple, listening to the teachers before He asked a single question. Listening is not silence — it is active, sustained reception. It gives the other person, and the Word, a place to stand. When we are not truly listening — when we are rehearsing our response — the Word falls and is lost.

Questions

Questions Build the Doors

"What do you seek?" John 1:38 · NKJV

Jesus' first recorded words in the Gospel of John were not a teaching or a declaration. They were a question. He then invited two strangers to come and see where He was staying. They came, remained with Him that day, and one of them recognized the Messiah. A question is a doorway — the asker steps back, and the answerer steps through.

Obedience

Obedience Is the Foundation

A beautiful room without a foundation is just decoration. Obedience is what holds the entire sacred space up. Jesus said the one who hears His words and does them is like a house built on rock — no flood can shake it. Every storytelling session in this training is built toward one moment: when the hearer says, even quietly in their own heart, "Yes, Lord."

In This Training

What You Will Discover

In this training, you will practice building this room — in your groups and in your own inner life. You will learn to hold silence after a question rather than filling it. You will practice listening without preparing your next sentence. And you will learn to ask the question that invites a hearer toward their own response to God.

The walls silent. The floor attentive. The doors asking. The foundation holding. "Yes, Lord."
