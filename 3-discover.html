"The entrance of Your words gives light; it gives understanding to the simple." — Psalm 119:130 · NKJV

Jesus asked approximately 339 questions in the Gospels. He was asked about 183 questions. He directly answered around 8 of them. That ratio is not an accident — and it is not merely a teaching technique. It is a window into how God has always revealed Himself: through encounter, through question, through the moment a person discovers something they could not have reasoned to alone.

The Finder Is the Owner

Truth You Discover Yourself, You Own

When a lawyer asked Jesus, "Who is my neighbor?" — Jesus did not answer. He told a story. He asked a question. He listened. Then He invited the lawyer to answer his own question. The lawyer answered. That truth belonged to the lawyer now — not to Jesus. The finder is the owner.

Truth you discover yourself is truth you own. And truth you own is truth you obey. When you tell people what to believe, you may produce agreement. When you lead them to discover it themselves, you produce conviction. Stories, examples, and questions trust the other person's capacity to reason, to feel, and to conclude — and that trust is itself a form of respect that opens people to being changed.

Stories

Stories Carry Truth Into the Light

"I will open My mouth in parables; I will utter things kept secret from the foundation of the world." — Matthew 13:35 · NKJV

God organized His Word predominantly as a story — not as systematic theology. Narrative is 43% of the entire Bible. Jesus spoke to crowds almost exclusively in parables, and about one-third of Jesus' recorded words in the Gospels are parables. Stories are the most ancient teaching and art forms, understood across ages and cultures, and especially powerful for oral learners — people who learn through listening and speaking rather than reading and writing. No one falls asleep during a good story.

But stories from God's Word do more than engage. They encourage discovery that leads to revelation. When Jesus said He spoke in parables to reveal things kept secret from the foundation of the world, He was not describing entertainment — He was describing revelation. A story creates the conditions in which the Spirit can illuminate what a hearer could not have seen through argument or proposition alone.

Examples

Examples Make the Invisible Visible

Jesus constantly drew from the world around Him — a woman sweeping her house, a father watching the road, a farmer scattering seed, a merchant searching for a fine pearl. These were not decorations on a lesson. They were the lesson. An example of Jesus takes what is unseen and eternal and plants it in something the hearer already knows and has touched. The abstract becomes concrete. The distant becomes near.

When eternal truth is clothed in the familiar — in a lost coin, a prodigal son, a mustard seed — it does not become less true. It becomes more accessible, more defensible, more memorable. Examples bridge between God's Word and human experience. They invite the hearer to say: I know this. I have lived something like this. And if that is true — then this must also be true.

Questions

Questions Open the Door

After Jesus asked a question, He was silent and listened. His follow-up always connected to what the other person said. A question is a doorway of discovery — the asker steps back, and the answerer steps through. When you ask a good question and hold the silence that follows, you give the Spirit room to lead that person to their own response. The answer that emerges from them is more powerful than any answer you could have given.

Discovery & Revelation

When You Find It, God Has Given It

Discovery through God's Word is never merely intellectual. When the Spirit uses a story, an example, or a question to lead a person to truth they could not have manufactured for themselves, something more than learning has happened — revelation has occurred. The psalmist named this precisely: the entrance of Your words gives light; it gives understanding to the simple. The Word enters. Light follows. Understanding is given.

This is why Jesus said, "He who has ears to hear, let him hear." The method creates the space; the Spirit provides the illumination. When Peter declared, "You are the Christ, the Son of the living God," Jesus did not say, Good reasoning. He said, "Flesh and blood has not revealed this to you, but My Father who is in heaven." (Matthew 16:17) The discovery was real. The revelation was God's own work.

When you use Jesus' discovery methods — story, example, question — you are not just teaching well. You are creating the conditions in which God can speak directly to a person through His own Word.

In This Training

What You Will Discover

Jesus was asked around 183 questions in the Gospels. He directly answered around 8. In this training, you will discover why that ratio was not accidental — it was revelation strategy. You will learn to tell Bible stories accurately, use examples that make invisible truth visible, and ask questions that open rather than close. Most importantly, you will discover for yourself the principle at the heart of this strategy: the finder is the owner. Truth people discover themselves is truth they own. And truth they own is truth they obey.

When you use Jesus' methods, expect God to reveal new things every time.
