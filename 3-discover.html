"The entrance of Your words gives light; it gives understanding to the simple." Psalm 119:130 · NKJV

Jesus asked approximately 339 questions in the Gospels. He directly answered about 8. That ratio is not an accident.

The Finder Is the Owner

Truth You Discover Yourself, You Own

When a lawyer asked Jesus, "Who is my neighbor?" — Jesus did not answer. He told a story. He asked a question. He listened. And then He invited the lawyer to answer his own question. The lawyer answered. That truth belonged to the lawyer now — not to Jesus. The finder is the owner. Truth you discover yourself is truth you are far more likely to obey.

This is Strategy 3: using stories, examples, and questions to lead people into their own discovery — the same method Jesus used everywhere He taught.

Stories

Stories Carry Truth Into the Light

"…revealing things kept secret from the foundation of the world." Matthew 13:35 · NKJV

God organized His Word predominantly as story — not as systematic theology. Narrative is 43% of the entire Bible. Jesus spoke to the crowds almost exclusively in parables. Stories are the most ancient teaching form, understood across ages and cultures. They are especially powerful for oral learners — people who learn through listening and speaking rather than reading and writing. No one falls asleep during a good story.

Questions

Questions Open the Door

After Jesus asked a question, He was silent and listened. His follow-up always connected to what the other person said. A question is a doorway — the asker steps back, and the answerer steps through. When you ask a good question and hold the silence that follows, you give the Spirit room to lead that person to their own response. The answer that emerges from them is more powerful than any answer you could have given.

Self-Discovery

Self-Discovery Creates Ownership

When you tell people what to believe, you may produce agreement. When you lead them to discover it themselves, you produce conviction. Stories, examples, and questions trust the other person's ability to reason, to feel, and to conclude. People allow themselves to be influenced by those who respect them enough to ask.

In This Training

What You Will Discover

In this training, you will practice telling Bible stories accurately and using discovery questions that open rather than close. You will learn to ask a question, fall silent, and watch what the Spirit does in the space that follows.
